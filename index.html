Mode 1 — How to play

Make a little map in your mind. Remember an old round. Then answer five questions.

1. Start small

Choose Mode 1, 1-back and 4 compass directions. Try a short session, such as 3 minutes. Leave Audio-only display off so you can see the words. Read this guide, then press Start.

Each round has three lines, also called statements. The first two are clues. The last line makes a claim.

Think of the letters as name tags on a map. “A is north of B” means A is above B.

Later, 8 directions add corners such as northeast: up and right. With 16, there are more directions between those. Use the exact direction shown.

2. Pick the right old round

N-back tells you how far to look back. Count whole rounds, not the lines inside them. During play, the old round is not shown beside the new one. Keep it in your memory.

1-backThe round just before this one.

2-backTwo rounds ago.

3-backThree rounds ago.

At 2-back, round 3 goes with round 1. Then round 4 goes with round 2. Remember the newest round too: you will need it later.

3. Keep one set of name tags

The letters may change. Old C might become new X. That is fine! But C must mean X in every line of this comparison. Two old letters cannot share one new name.

Use one set of name changes that makes as many lines match as possible. Do not pick a different set for each line.

  • The first two clues may swap places.
  • The last line must match the old last line.
  • “A is north of B” = “B is south of A.” Saying it backward is okay only when the direction flips too.

Remember the pattern of directions, not just the letters you see.

4. Give all five answers

The buttons show letters. Tap the button, or press its key if Keyboard controls is on.

Question 4 is different. Use only the two clues on the screen now. Follow both clues from one end of the chain to the other. If they link A with B, then B with C, the two ends are A and C.

The last line must give the correct direction between those two end letters. Repeating just one clue is not enough. If the last line uses the wrong pair, is wrong, or is not proved by the clues, choose L.

Each answer locks in as soon as you choose it. You get feedback straight away. Finish all five; the next round then starts by itself.

5. Let’s solve one together

Imagine this old round is the one you need to remember.

The old round

  1. A is north of B.
  2. B is north of C.
  3. A is north of C.

The round on screen now

  1. A is north of B.
  2. B is north of X.
  3. A is south of X.

Our name key: A stays A. B stays B. C becomes X.

  1. Line 1 matches. Choose Yes: A.
  2. Line 2 matches after C becomes X. Choose Yes: D.
  3. Line 3 changed from north to south. Choose No: J.
  4. Start at X. Go up to B, then up again to A. A is north of X, so the last line is wrong. Choose No: L.
  5. Only two lines match. The whole round does not. Choose No: N.

All five answers: A, D, J, L, N.

“Same as before” does not mean “true”. Suppose the old last line also said “A is south of C”. Both rounds would now have the same wrong claim. The whole round would match, but the claim would still not follow from the clues. You would choose L for question 4 and Space for question 5.

6. What about the first few rounds?

During the first N rounds, there is no old target yet. At 1-back, that means round 1. At 2-back, it means rounds 1 and 2.

Choose S, F, J and N for the four memory questions. Still check today’s clues and choose K or L for question 4.

These answers count. You must finish all five even on the first round.

7. Take your time and learn

There is no timer for each answer, but the session clock keeps running. Pause takes a break; Stop ends the session. With keyboard controls on, P pauses or resumes and Esc stops.

A round is fully correct only when all five answers are right. Two matching lines do not make a whole match. Close-looking rounds are part of the game.

Want an easier start? Keep 1-back and 4 directions. The Cognitive interference setting is fixed; it cannot be lowered. Stop before changing your mode, N-back level or compass directions.

Mode 2 — How to play

Look past the extra words. Do all three map lines repeat the pattern from the right old round?

1. Start small

Choose Mode 2, 1-back and 4 compass directions. Try a short session, such as 3 minutes. Leave Audio-only display off so you can see the words. Read this guide, then press Start.

Each round has three lines. The first two are clues. The last is a claim. The game calls the group of three a triad.

Think of letters as name tags on a map. “A is north of B” means A is above B.

North
↑ UpWest
← Left●East
Right →↓ Down
South

Eight directions add corners such as northeast: up and right. Sixteen add more directions between those. A nearby direction is not the same direction.

2. Remember the right round

N-back tells you how far to look back. Count whole rounds, not lines. During play, the old round is not shown beside the new one. Keep it in your memory.

1-backThe round just before this one.

2-backTwo rounds ago.

3-backThree rounds ago.

At 2-back, compare round 3 with round 1. Then compare round 4 with round 2. Keep remembering each new round for later.

The first N rounds are for watching and remembering. At 1-back, watch round 1. At 2-back, watch rounds 1 and 2.

Do not answer these setup rounds. The buttons stay off, the game moves on by itself, and these rounds do not count toward your score.

3. Find the little map clues

You might see: Inner Completion A is north of B.

For your answer, focus on: A is north of B.

Inner, Outer, Completion, Multiplication and the other extra words are labels. They do not change whether you should press Match. You do not need to multiply or divide anything.

The letters may change too. Make one name key for all three lines. If old A becomes new X, keep using X for A everywhere. Give each old letter its own new name.

4. Try a matching round

Imagine the old round below is exactly N rounds back.

The old round

  1. Inner Completion A is north of B.
  2. Outer Multiplication B is north of C.
  3. Difference A is north of C.

The round on screen now

  1. Outer Division X is north of Y.
  2. Inner Action Y is north of Z.
  3. Connection X is north of Z.

Our name key: A becomes X. B becomes Y. C becomes Z.

All three lines keep the same pattern. The labels changed, but the answer did not.

Choose Match.

Now change only the new last line: “Connection X is east of Z.”

That direction used to be north. The whole pattern no longer matches. Choose No Match. Two matching lines are not enough.

5. Watch for a different way of saying it

  • The first two clues may swap places.
  • “A is north of B” = “B is south of A.” Both describe the same map. The direction must flip when the names swap ends.
  • The last line must still match the old last line. You cannot swap it with one of the first two clues.
  • All three lines must work with the same name key.

You are checking memory, not proving the last line. A wrong claim can be repeated exactly. If the whole three-line pattern repeats, choose Match even if that last claim is not proved by the first two clues.

6. Give just one answer

Wait until the speech finishes and the answer buttons become available.

Match

All three lines match the right old round together.

Tap Match.
Keyboard: F or J.

No Match

The whole pattern does not match. Even one map link that does not fit means No Match.

Tap No Match.
Keyboard: D or K.

Keys work when Keyboard controls is on. After your answer, the game shows feedback and moves to the next round.

There is no timer for each answer, but the session clock keeps running. Pause takes a break; Stop ends the session. With keyboard controls on, P pauses or resumes and Esc stops.

Close-looking rounds are part of the game. Cognitive interference is fixed. To make learning easier, stay at 1-back and 4 directions. Stop before changing your mode, N-back level or compass directions.

How to Train

Mode 1 — Relational Conflict Matrix

Choose 4, 8 or 16 compass directions before starting Mode 1. Four uses the cardinal directions, eight adds the diagonals, and sixteen adds the intermediate directions. Finer resolution increases directional precision. Cognitive interference changes lure difficulty independently of compass resolution.

Response Window (seconds per trial) has been removed. Each trial remains available until all five relevant decisions are entered. Because each trial requires several inputs, a fixed deadline could corrupt the training by mixing relational reasoning with externally imposed speed pressure. Immediate feedback after each input lets each user coordinate speed with their own processing rate, judge whether to speed up or slow down, and complete the full decision set without being rushed. The overall session-length control still determines when the session ends.

Answer five questions on every trial: whether each of the three statements matches a statement in the trial N positions earlier, whether Statement 3 is logically entailed by Statements 1 and 2, and whether the complete three-statement structure matches. Statement matches require one globally consistent mapping of letters and one-to-one statement assignment. Reversed wording counts only when the compass direction is inverted correctly. A single or partial match never makes the whole triad a match.

At high cognitive-interference settings, non-match trials are deliberately constructed as close structural lures: one or two statements may match the N-back target while a precise direction, role assignment or entailment relation prevents the complete triad from matching. At maximum, the generator strongly favours two-of-three statement near-misses across 4-, 8- and 16-direction sessions.

Keyboard mapping: Statement 1 A/S, Statement 2 D/F, Statement 3 H/J, within-trial entailment K/L, and complete-triad N-back Spacebar/N. In every pair, the left key is the positive response and the right key is the negative response. G is deliberately unused.

Best regards to all persons who helped with Brain Workshop.

Mode 2 — Ontological Integration

Mode 2 also requires 4, 8 or 16 compass directions. Compare the complete triad with the trial exactly N positions earlier; its first N trials are unscored setup trials. Responses are self-paced.
